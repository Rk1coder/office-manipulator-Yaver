# Narrative: build the deck as an argument, not a list of topics

## Arc
1. **Hook (1 slide):** why this matters to *this* audience — a striking number, a question, or a tension.
2. **Context (1–2):** the situation as it is today; only what the audience needs to follow the rest.
3. **Core (most slides):** the substance, one idea per slide, ordered so each slide prepares the next.
4. **Implications (1–2):** what follows — impact, recommendation, decision, next steps.
5. **Close (1):** the single takeaway the audience should repeat afterwards.

## Slide titles are claims
- Write each title as the slide's conclusion ("Koloniler görev paylaşımıyla 10 kat verimli çalışır"),
  not its topic ("Görev Paylaşımı"). Reading only the titles in order must tell the whole story.
- Titles: one line, ≤ 60 characters when possible, sentence case, no trailing period.

## One idea per slide
- If a slide needs "and" to describe its purpose, split it.
- Every slide answers one question; the outline's `summary` says which.
- Move detail the speaker will *say* into `speakerNotes`, not onto the slide.

## Evidence over adjectives
- Prefer a number, a comparison, an example or a quote from the source to a general statement.
- Use the source material's own facts; never invent statistics. When a number is computed, say from what.

## Rhythm
- Alternate dense and light slides: after two content-heavy slides, give a BIG_NUMBER, QUOTE or
  BIG_STATEMENT breather.
- Mark parts with SECTION_DIVIDER only for decks of 10+ slides.
- Plan `imageNeeded` only where a picture carries meaning (a place, an object, a process the
  audience hasn't seen) — not as decoration. Image prompts describe subject, composition,
  style and mood, and ask for no text inside the image.

## Language
- Turkish decks: natural, correct Turkish with full diacritics (ç ğ ı İ ö ş ü); keep English technical
  terms in parentheses on first use. Avoid translated-sounding phrasing.
